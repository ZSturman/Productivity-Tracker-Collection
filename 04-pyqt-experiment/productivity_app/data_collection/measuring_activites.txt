* Extremely Productive: Activities that directly contribute to major goals and involve high mental or physical engagement.
* Very Productive: Activities that contribute significantly to goals but may not involve as much engagement.
* Moderately Productive: Activities that contribute to goals but at a lower intensity or frequency.
* Slightly Productive: Activities that contribute to goals indirectly or to a small extent.
* Neutral: Activities that neither contribute to nor detract from goals.
* Slightly Unproductive: Activities that slightly detract from goals, or are neutral but consume significant time or resources.
* Moderately Unproductive: Activities that detract from goals and consume moderate time or resources.
* Very Unproductive: Activities that significantly detract from goals and consume considerable time or resources.
* Extremely Unproductive: Activities that detract greatly from goals and consume excessive time or resources. 



Measuring productivity quantitatively can be challenging due to the inherent subjectivity of the concept. However, there are several approaches that you could take:
1. Time Tracking: You could measure the amount of time spent on each activity. This is a simple and objective measure, but it does not account for variations in the intensity or quality of work.
2. Goal Completion: You could set specific, measurable goals for each activity (for example, "complete X tasks", "write Y lines of code", "read Z pages"), and then measure productivity based on the extent to which these goals are achieved.
3. Output Produced: Depending on the nature of the activity, you could measure productivity based on the output produced (for example, the number of bugs fixed, the number of documents reviewed, the number of lessons completed).
4. Performance Metrics: For certain types of work, there may be specific performance metrics that can be used to measure productivity. For example, in software development, you might use metrics like "lines of code per hour" or "bugs per thousand lines of code".
5. Rating Scales: You could use a rating scale to quantify subjective perceptions of productivity. For example, you might rate each activity on a scale of 1-10 based on how productive you felt while doing it. This method is subjective and can vary between individuals, but it can provide a quantitative measure of perceived productivity.
6. Task Completion: You could also consider the number of tasks or sub-tasks completed during a certain period as a measure of productivity. This could be particularly useful for administrative or multi-tasking activities.
